Research · Crypto Derivatives

Prediction Markets Fair-Value Engine

  • What it is: A fair-value engine for 15-minute crypto Up/Down markets. They settle on the last 60-second average versus the window open. It prices them in real time with a Gaussian CDF: P(Up) = Φ((spot − open) / (σ × time left)) — how far price is from the open, given vol and time left, as a probability from 0 to 1.
  • How it prices them: Volatility is the main source of edge. Instead of a trailing average, a LightGBM model trained on ~2.1M rows of 1-minute BTC/ETH/SOL/XRP data (~5 years, 73 features) forecasts the next 15 minutes of volatility once a minute. Trees spit vol, not direction; every second the same formula turns that into P(Up).
  • What it also gives you: A live trading terminal that pulls second-by-second prices from several exchanges (Polymarket, Coinbase, Binance) and shows fair value on screen [P(Up) in the top right] for fast decisions. — terminal

Library · Derivatives

Lookback Options Pricing Library

  • What it is: A code library for pricing "lookback options", exotic options whose payoff depends on the highest or lowest price the underlying asset hit during its life, not just its price at expiry. It covers six variants (floating-strike, fixed-strike, reverse-strike, limited-risk, partial, and quanto/currency-linked).
  • How it prices them: Uses closed-form math formulas (Black-Scholes-style) where possible, and falls back to Monte Carlo simulation for the more complex variants that don't have a clean formula. It also adjusts for realistic details like dividends and the fact that real-world monitoring happens at discrete intervals (e.g. daily closes) rather than continuously.
  • What it also gives you: Risk sensitivities (Delta, Gamma, Vega — how the option's price moves as the stock price or volatility changes) for every variant, plus a built-in validation suite to check the pricing math is behaving correctly.

Research · Rates

Negative 30-Year European Swap Spreads

  • What it is: Euro 30-year swap rates have traded below German government bond yields since 2022 — an inversion of what theory predicts, since swaps should normally carry a premium over "risk-free" sovereign debt.
  • Why it happens: Massive, regulation-driven hedging demand from European pension funds (Solvency II) floods the swap market and pushes swap rates down, while a scarcity of long-dated Bunds keeps bond yields from falling in tandem.
  • Why it doesn't get arbitraged away: Bank capital rules (Basel III leverage ratio) and repo market financing costs make the theoretical arbitrage trade unprofitable in practice, so the gap persists as a structural feature rather than a correctable mispricing.
